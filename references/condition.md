# Photos and condition

Classify from photos, never from listing copy. Agent copy is a claim; a photo is
evidence. Both are weaker than a walkthrough, and the report says which one it used.

## What to inspect, when the photos exist

| Area | Look for |
|---|---|
| Kitchen | Cabinet fronts and boxes, counters, backsplash, appliances, soffits, floor |
| Bathrooms | Tile and grout, tub/shower surround, vanity, fixtures, water staining |
| Flooring | Coverage, transitions, gaps, cupping, patched or missing sections |
| Walls and ceilings | Cracks, water rings, peeling paint, patched drywall, sagging |
| Windows | Single vs double pane, sash condition, storms, boarded, painted shut |
| Doors | Interior slabs, hardware, alignment, missing |
| Interior finishes | Trim, casing, hardware, lighting fixtures, closet systems |
| Roof and exterior | Shingle curl/lift/missing tabs, flashing, valleys, chimney, gutters |
| Siding | Rot, peeling paint, missing pieces, vegetation against the wall |
| Foundation warning signs | Stair-step brick cracks, spalling, settlement lines, uneven porch |
| Yard | Grading toward the house, standing water, debris, overgrowth, access |

**One exterior photo can only support an exterior finding.** It cannot support a
kitchen estimate.

## The five classifications

1. **Turnkey** — move-in. Cosmetic only, no visible defect. *Reject for wholesale.*
2. **Light renovation** — paint, flooring, minor kitchen/bath, cosmetic exterior.
3. **Medium renovation** — full kitchen and baths, flooring throughout, some
   mechanicals, partial exterior.
4. **Heavy renovation** — multiple systems plus cosmetic plus structural items.
5. **Unknown / insufficient evidence** — photos missing, incomplete, or unreadable.

Classification is `ESTIMATED` from visible evidence and is never a professional
condition assessment.

## Three separate statements — required in every report

**A. What the photos show.** Only observable facts: "cabinet doors missing in the
kitchen photo; the bathroom tile is intact; roof shingles appear lifted at the right
rake."

**B. What may reasonably be inferred.** With the inference named as such: "the missing
cabinets and the 1970s soffit suggest the kitchen has not been touched, consistent
with a full kitchen replacement."

**C. What cannot be confirmed remotely.** Always includes the systems and structure:
roof deck and remaining life, foundation and structural integrity, electrical service
and wiring, plumbing supply and drain, HVAC condition and age, sewer line, mold,
asbestos, lead paint, water intrusion history, and the interior of any room not
photographed.

**Never write that a photo shows a roof, foundation, electrical, plumbing, HVAC, or
structural component is sound.** An appearance in a photograph is not a condition
report.

## Flag, don't diagnose

When evidence exists for a potential structural, water, fire, mold, electrical,
plumbing, or roof problem, flag it with the photo it came from and price it into the
contingency (`repairs.md`). Do not diagnose. "Possible active water intrusion at the
ceiling corner, second photo" is right. "Failed roof" is not.

## Remote verification — required for anything beyond POTENTIAL

The seller is in Nigeria. For any property being seriously considered, name the
specific path to current visual evidence:

| Source | What to ask for | Realistic when |
|---|---|---|
| Listing agent | Full photo set, any interior video, showing access, utility/roof age, seller situation | Any on-market property. Fastest, and free |
| Seller or occupant | Phone video walkthrough, specific close-ups on request | Off-market and FSBO |
| Authorised local representative | Paid walkthrough with a photo checklist and a video call | Heavy or structural-risk properties |
| Contractor | Itemised bid on the specific items you flagged | Before any contract on a medium/heavy property |
| County records | Permits, violations, prior sale, tax status | Always, in parallel |

A property with no path to current photos is `POTENTIAL` at best, and the report states
which path is being pursued and what it would take to close the gap.

## Photo checklist to send to an agent or representative

Front elevation · both side elevations · rear · roof (from street, and any low-angle) ·
kitchen · every bathroom · each bedroom · basement or crawlspace · electrical panel ·
furnace and water heater · attic access · any room with visible damage · the yard.
