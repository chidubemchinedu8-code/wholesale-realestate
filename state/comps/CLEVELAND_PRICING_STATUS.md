## 3. The arithmetic finding — why the rest will not clear

Grounding: the one measured comp set in this market, **44102 West Boulevard, $121.25/sf
renovated**. A 1,200 sf finished house ARVs at **~$145,500**.

To pay an ask **and** a $10,000 assignment fee, the ARV must exceed `(ask + fee + repairs) / 0.70`:

| ask | ARV needed, LIGHT | ARV needed, MODERATE | ARV needed, HEAVY |
|---|---|---|---|
| $75,000 | $155,714 | **$181,429** | $207,143 |
| $85,000 | $170,000 | **$195,714** | $221,429 |
| $95,000 | $184,286 | **$210,000** | $235,714 |
| $112,000 | $208,571 | **$234,286** | $260,000 |
| $150,000 | $262,857 | **$288,571** | $314,286 |

**A $100,000 ask at MODERATE repairs needs a $217,143 ARV — $181/sf. The market here is
$121/sf.** Every shortlist row asks between $75,000 and $195,000, and the cheap ones are cheap
because they are small: 990 sf, 1,026 sf, 1,081 sf, 1,102 sf, 1,131 sf, 1,160 sf.

The cap is not the ask. **It is that `0.70 × a $121/sf ARV` cannot absorb a $40,000 repair bill,
a $10,000 fee, and a five-figure ask simultaneously.** At MODERATE the ceiling is
`$84,875 − $40,425 = $44,450` on a 1,000 sf subject. That is the whole budget — before any fee.

**Consequence:** the assignment model does not clear on standard MLS inventory in these hoods.
It can only clear where the seller is **far below** retail — off-market, distressed, or a
motivated/absentee seller. That is a sourcing problem, not a comping problem, and it is exactly
what `references/off-market.md` exists for.

## 4. Two hard caveats on all of the above

1. **n=3.** The ARV rests on three comps because that is all 44102 West Boulevard offers in
   12 months of renovated, in-band sales. The engine prints a warning for n=3 and it is earned.
2. **±40% is $112,033 wide** on a $140,041 ARV. At the bottom of that band this deal is worth
   $84,000 and the MAO is $8,392. The band, not the point, is the honest figure.
