## Honest caveats

1. **Bath contradiction** — 1 vs 2 baths is unresolved and it moves repair scope.
2. **ARV is above the ZIP.** Entry ARV $229,647 vs the 43224 average of ~$196,618. The comps
   support it — they are all recent, all renovated, all within 0.34 mi with a 3.1% spread — but
   it assumes the subject reaches *their* finish level, not just "well-maintained."
3. **All three comps are flips.** That is what the method wants (renovated-condition ARV), but
   it means the ARV is a *post-rehab retail* number, not a market-clearing number for the house
   as it stands today.
4. **Coordinates are approximate.** Nominatim returns street centerlines here, not parcels;
   3001 Woodland's centerline point landed 0.8 mi south in Arlington Park, the wrong hood
   entirely. Comp distances (0.08–0.34 mi) are therefore estimates within roughly ±0.1 mi. That
   does not change a TIGHT verdict, but it is D-6 territory and is flagged rather than hidden.
5. **Photographs are partly virtually staged** — condition claims are weaker than usual.

## The point of the exercise

The comping works: a real hood feed, three renovated sales, every gate passing on evidence, a
3.1% spread. The ARV is defensible. **The deal is not** — and that gap is the whole job. A
correct ARV on a lead that cannot be contracted is a *screened-out* lead, not a lost one.
