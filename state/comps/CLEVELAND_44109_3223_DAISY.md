## 1. Why it is unpriced — three independent reasons

### (a) Bed-count floor cannot be met
Zillow lists **5 beds**. A 5-bed subject takes **5-bed-and-up comps only** (`check_7_bed_floor`,
no exceptions). Refining 44109's entire 6-month house pool to ≥1,600 sf — 24 sales in total —
yields exactly **two** 5-bed sales: 3100 Archwood (2,109 sf) and 3891 W 21st (1,641 sf). Two is
not three.

### (b) The sqft band fails even before the bed floor
Subject is **2,334 sf**; ±30% is **1,634–3,034 sf**. Of the 24 large sales, only five sit in that
band: 3287 W 17th (2,232), 4762 S Hills (2,260), 3622 Denison (2,344), 3100 Archwood (2,109),
4589 S Hills (2,057). Five candidates across **all of 44109**.

### (c) Of those five, the renovated ones do not survive the same-neighbourhood rule
| comp | sf | price | condition | disposition |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 3287 W 17th St | 2,232 | $220,000 | "thoughtfully transformed", duplex→SFR | renovated, but **Buckeye–Shaker**, 1.3 mi |
| 4762 S Hills Dr | 2,260 | $245,000 | "updated Cape Cod" | renovated, but **Old Brooklyn**, 1.5 mi |
| 3622 Denison Ave | 2,344 | $80,000 | "spacious layout **offers a great opportunity**" | **dated** — barred from ARV |
| 4589 S Hills Dr | 2,057 | $305,000 | "charming… **original** architectural details" | **maintained** — barred |
| 3100 Archwood Ave | 2,109 | $289,000 | **"Entered for comp purposes."** | **condition unknown** — barred |

**3100 Archwood is the killer.** $137/sf, 5bd, in band, and the remark is literally *"Entered for
comp purposes"* — a builder/agent entry with **zero condition information**. An unknown-condition
sale cannot enter a renovated-only ARV pool (rule 7). Its very existence is also a warning: at
$289,000 it is **2.4×** the county appraised value, so it is either a genuine high-end renovation
or an unusual exit. Either way it cannot be used without a walkthrough.

**What is left is two renovated comps in two different hoods, neither of which is the subject's.**
Pooling Old Brooklyn and Buckeye–Shaker onto a Clark-Fulton subject to reach n=3 is exactly the
D-31 defect. Recommended rather than borrowed.

Same street, 2026-07-24: **3618 Daisy Ave, 4bd/1.5ba, 1,373 sf, sold $76,000 = $55.35/sf.**
That is a **dated** sale, so it sets the as-is floor and never the ARV.

If the subject's finished value were merely the renovated 44109 large-home band — 3287 W 17th at
$98.57/sf and 4762 S Hills at $108.41/sf — then 2,334 sf × ~$103/sf ≈ **$240,000** *pointing*.
**That is not an ARV and must not be quoted.** It is stated only to show the size of the gap the
method is refusing to paper over: a real number here could plausibly be double the county's
$94,600 appraisal, and an invented one could be half of it.

**The honest position: this may be a very good deal or not a deal at all, and the data cannot yet
tell us which.** That is the answer.

## 4. Correction to my own earlier work

The lead table I produced last turn carried a `mao_moderate` for every row, computed as
`sf × (0.70 × $121.25 − $35) − $10,000`. **$121.25/sf is the 44102 West Boulevard median.** Every
lead in that table is **44109 or 44111**. Under protocol §3 a comp may never be borrowed across a
hood, so those MAO figures were invalid and have been **retracted to `UNSOURCED`** in
`cuyahoga_foreclosure_leads_2026-11-08.csv`.

The *thesis* from last turn survives — MAO scales as `sf × (0.70 × hood_$psf − repair_$psf) − fee`,
so big-and-cheap beats small-and-cheap. The **number** did not survive, because it plugged in
another hood's $/sf. A 44109 figure now requires a 44109 comp set, and this file is the evidence
that building one takes real work.
